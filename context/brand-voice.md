# Brand Voice

Use this file to capture how the company should sound.

## Tone

- Overall tone: Warm, respectful, trustworthy, and practical — like a thoughtful family advisor, not a dating-app brand
- Level of technical depth: Low. Explain features and processes in plain, everyday language
- How opinionated the writing should be: Moderately opinionated — clearly positioned against anonymous, swipe-style matrimony apps, without attacking competitors by name
- What the brand should feel like: Serious about marriage as an institution; accountable; calm; family-friendly; founder-led — plain, confident, mission-driven ("access to serious matrimony should not be gated by payment")

## Writing Preferences

- Paragraph style: Short paragraphs, scannable, concrete
- Sentence style: Clear and direct; plain words over jargon
- CTA style: Invitational and low-pressure (create a profile, start your network, request verification) — tied to the next logical step, not hype
- Jargon guidance: Avoid industry jargon; define product terms (Connector, Candidate, Digital ID Verification) in plain language
- Things to avoid: Hype and guarantees, pressure tactics, swipe-culture references, sarcasm, anything casual or flippant about marriage

## Vocabulary

- Preferred terms: Candidate, Connector, trusted introduction, network, verification, privacy controls, intentional, biodata
- Terms to avoid: Dating, swiping, "matches" described as auto-generated (unless explaining what we don't do), soulmate-type promises, describing verification as required or mandatory
- Product naming conventions: "Panosia Connect"; roles capitalized (Candidate, Connector); verification levels referenced as Level-1/2/3; feature names such as "Digital ID Verification" treated as proper nouns

## Proof Style

- What kinds of evidence are acceptable: Platform features and mechanics (verification process, privacy controls, relationship labels); brand first-person posts ("why we built this"); clearly illustrative example personas (e.g., Ryan and Sarah) for explanation
- Whether to use first-person experience: Yes, in brand voice — "why we created Panosia Connect" style posts are on-brand
- Whether fictional examples are allowed: Yes, clearly illustrative personas are fine
- Statistical claims: Only with user-provided data; never invent numbers

## Messaging Priorities

- Core themes to emphasize: Trust before matches; family and community are part of the process; privacy stays in the user's control; verification reduces risk; free to join with no subscriptions — access to opportunity is not the product; serious lifelong commitment only, not casual dating
- Important caveats to acknowledge: Verification reduces but cannot guarantee against fraud; no platform can guarantee a match; the online process still depends on real-world judgment; verification is optional and paid — never frame it as required to use the platform
- Claims to avoid unless supported: Success rates, user counts, fraud-reduction statistics, absolute "safe/safest" claims, invented conversion stats (existing site copy mentions "3x more profile responses" for background checks — reuse only that wording, don't invent new numbers)
